How to run a feedback review your whole team will join

By Sara Rahman·Oct 28, 2026·6 min read

Most teams agree that customer feedback matters. Far fewer have a habit that turns it into decisions. The fix isn’t another dashboard: it’s a 30-minute meeting, once a week, that product, support and marketing actually want to attend.

Here’s the format we use at Signalry, and the one our customers copy most often.

1. Start with what changed, not everything

Nobody wants to scroll through 400 tickets. Open with the three themes that grew most since last week, and one that shrank. Change is what makes people lean in.

  • Top three growing themes, with counts
  • One theme that went down, and why
  • One quote that says it better than a chart

2. Put a number on every theme

“Customers are confused by billing” starts a debate. “42 customers, $310k in ARR, mentioned billing this month” starts a plan. Attach customer counts and revenue to every theme you discuss.

The meeting got shorter the day we started showing revenue next to each theme. Nobody argues with the number.

3. Leave with owners, not opinions

End with a short list: each item gets an owner and a date. Product takes the roadmap items, support takes the help articles and macros, marketing takes the quotes worth sharing.

  1. Decide: fix, build, explain or ignore
  2. Assign one owner per item
  3. Check last week’s items first next time

4. Close the loop with customers

When something ships, tell the customers who asked for it. It takes minutes with a saved list, and it’s the single best way to get more feedback next time.

Try it for four weeks. If the meeting isn’t worth the time by then, change the format. In our experience, it will be.

Sara Rahman

VP Customer Success at Signalry. Sara has run feedback programmes at three SaaS companies and still reads every NPS comment.
